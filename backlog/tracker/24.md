# #24 — Watch a card and get notified when it changes

## Ask

Hap, on the [[2026-09-08]] call, looking for a way to flag priority on a card without chasing it verbally:

> Hap: *"Is there… is there any way I can click on something, and you guys get notified that… you know, like… if I said express interest. Or, like, Hap's priority."*

Joao named the precedent and committed to it live:

> Joao: *"Fuck, that's such a good idea. Other systems, like Jira and Trello, they had something called Watch. Which means that you are watching this issue. And usually people get notified like, hey, Hap is watching this issue."*

> Joao: *"So, I will make a point to add a watch feature like Jira, so you can do that. And we didn't actually have the ability to send emails until, I think, Sunday, but now we do, so I'll make sure to send an email for when you watch, put things to watch. So there will be a little eye icon, like an eye, to indicate that you are watching that. And maybe we can have a page of only your watched stuff."*

> Hap: *"Yeah, exactly. Yeah, the stuff that I'm watching, right?"*

## Status

**Done — implementation reviewed and accepted.** The two watch migrations have been applied to production and recorded in migration history; the app ships with the #26 release.

The implementation includes an eye control on board cards, browser notifications for watching and watched-card movement, default-on email preferences with individual and check-all controls, and a "My watched issues" folder below the projects on the signed-in projects page. Project board tiles use three columns on desktop and adapt to smaller screens. Notification emails use the Cardstock paper layout with a folder tab, serif heading, and clear card link.

Watch-start emails go to project members and the site owner, including the person watching. Movement emails go to watchers other than the actor. Browser and email preferences are independent. Lane-change triggers cover UI, bulk moves, and CLI sync; queued mail rechecks preferences, watching, and access before delivery.

Verified the production build, TypeScript and changed-file lint, 16 mail/preference tests, 16 CLI integration tests, nine browser tests, database access and queue tests, and the scheduler setup locally. Desktop and mobile layouts reviewed, including the email template. Automated mail tests used mocks; the owner's queued watch notification was subsequently sent successfully through the configured mail service during manual verification.

## Residuals

- Scheduled email retries still require the Vercel `CRON_SECRET` and matching one-time Supabase Cron setup in `docs/deploy.md`. This workspace has no Vercel credentials to configure or verify those settings. The mail key was confirmed by the owner; app-triggered delivery is included in the release.
