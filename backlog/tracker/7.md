# #7 — The lane that needs you is off-screen behind a horizontal scroll

## Ask

The [[2026-09-01]] call, immediately after Hap asked how to see what was waiting on him:

> Hap: *"So, how do I, A, look at all the things that need Hap, right?"*

Getting him there took six turns of directions:

> Joao: *"So if you scroll to the left or to the right… There should be a scroll bar below. Yep… All the way, yep, needs a path, right there. So, no, no, no, you scroll too much… Yeah, right there. Needs help."*

Joao offered the fix in the moment:

> Joao: *"So happy. And if you want, we can put this one right at the left side so you don't have to scroll."*

## Status

**Shape decided, not started.** The half that blocked it is done: [[10]] made every
lane draggable, so Joao's in-call offer — put that lane on the left — is now one
gesture instead of impossible. What remains is the per-viewer half.

## Rationale

A waiting lane exists to prompt a particular person, so its value is a function of
how easily that person can see it. Sitting eighth from the left behind a horizontal
scroll bar inverts that: the lane that most needs attention is the one that takes
the most work to reach.

The card originally posed this as a choice — move the lane globally, or build a
per-viewer answer — on the grounds that the lane that matters differs per person.
Joao's ruling on 2026-09-04 is that it is not a choice, and that the premise was
half wrong:

> *"scrum and kanban are collaborative stuff. if people cannot agree on lane order
> they shouldn't be messing with software engineering."*

So lane order stays **global and shared**, and that is a feature rather than a
limitation: a board whose columns mean different things to different people is not
a board. There is no conflict to resolve, no per-viewer override of position, and
no reconciliation between the two.

The per-person need is real but it is a different need — not "where does this lane
live" but "what do I always want in front of me". That gets its own action:

- **Move lane** — drag, global, already built in [[10]]. Changes the board for
  everyone, immediately, because `lanes.position` is a shared column in the
  realtime publication.
- **Pin lane** — per viewer, held in the browser. A pinned lane is always in view
  for the person who pinned it, regardless of where the shared order puts it.

Two actions, two scopes, no overlap. The pin does not reorder anything and the move
does not follow anybody home.

## Residuals

- **Where the pin lives.** Decided: the browser. Worth knowing that the board
  already keeps a per-viewer lane setting server-side — collapsed/min/max state
  lives in `members.prefs.laneViews`, keyed by board id — so browser storage means
  two similar per-person lane settings in two different places, and a pin that does
  not follow someone from desktop to laptop. Cheaper and needs no migration; the
  cost is paid by whoever opens the board somewhere new.
- **What "always in" means at the edges.** A pinned lane that is also collapsed, or
  one pinned on a board whose owner later deletes it, both want an answer. So does
  more than one pin at once — an order among pins, or a cap of one.
- **Whether Hap still has the problem.** The board this was raised against had ten
  lanes; the default is now four. The horizontal fold that caused the complaint may
  simply not exist any more, which is worth asking him before building rather than
  after.
- **The filter reading.** Hap's literal words were *"look at all the things that
  need Hap"* — which an assignee filter answers more directly than any lane
  position. Assignee support shipped after this card was written. A pin and a filter
  are not the same feature and it is worth knowing which he actually wanted.
